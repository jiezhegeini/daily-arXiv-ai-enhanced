Abstract: The short-time Fourier transform (STFT) characterizes the local frequency content of time-varying signals by evaluating the discrete Fourier transform over a sliding analysis window. The STFT is governed by a fundamental resolution trade-off: a short window localizes rapid transients in time at the cost of frequency resolution, whereas a long window resolves closely spaced harmonics at the expense of temporal resolution. The Universal Short-Time Fourier Transform (U-STFT) resolves this trade-off sequentially by modeling the analysis window duration as a data-adaptive state variable that indexes the number of hops included in the current observation interval. A window length trellis aggregates the resulting $\mathcal{O}(3^M)$ candidate window schedules with linear complexity per frame, guaranteeing a cumulative mix-loss regret bound of $\mathcal{O}((k+1)\log M)$ relative to any hindsight window schedule with $k$ resolution changes over $M$ frames. Evaluations on synthetic and marine bioacoustic signals demonstrate adaptive resolution tracking, enhanced spectral concentration, and exact time-domain invertibility.
