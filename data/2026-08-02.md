### [1] [Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning for Base Station Placement in TDOA-Based Localization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28002)
*Bastian Perner,Pratik Gajanan Raut,Maximilian Lübke,Norman Franchi*

Main category: eess.SP

TL;DR: 针对复杂无线环境下传统基站部署方法忽略环境干扰的问题，本文提出了一种基于射线追踪数据和多智能体强化学习的部署框架，在提升定位精度方面表现出优于传统几何学方法的潜力。

Motivation: 传统的基于几何学的方法（如GDOP）忽略了现实中非视距（NLOS）阴影和多径效应导致的到达时间（TOA）偏差，限制了其在复杂5G/6G环境中的定位性能，因此需要一种能感知环境影响的基站部署方法。

Method: 提出了一种基于射线追踪（Ray-Tracing）辅助的多智能体强化学习（MARL）框架。利用近端策略优化（PPO）算法，智能体通过学习基于3D校园模型生成的信道冲激响应（CIR）数据进行协同基站部署，并在优化过程中综合考量定位精度与覆盖范围。

Result: 实验表明，该方法在平均定位平均绝对误差（MAE）上比强几何基准方法降低了约3%。在特定测试路段上，最高可提升约14%的定位精度，证明了该方法在处理复杂传播环境时的优越性。

Conclusion: 将特定场景的传播数据整合到基站部署流程中，能够匹配甚至在特定环境下优于传统的几何部署策略。该研究验证了利用环境感知数据进行智能部署的潜力，并为实现更稳健的无线网络规划提供了方向。

Abstract: Accurate localization of devices is a key capability for emerging 5G and 6G networks and depends on effective base station (BS) placement. Conventional geometry-based approaches such as Geometric Dilution of Precision (GDOP) ignore realistic propagation effects such as Non-Line of Sight (NLOS) shadowing and multipath-induced Time of Arrival (TOA) bias caused by buildings. This paper proposes a ray-tracing-assisted Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL) framework for environment-aware BS placement in Time Difference of Arrival (TDOA) localization systems. Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) agents are trained on Channel Impulse Responses (CIRs) generated from a detailed 3D model of a university campus. Each agent cooperatively places one BS while optimizing a shared reward that combines localization accuracy and coverage. The approach is evaluated on five campus segments with varying propagation characteristics. Results show that the learned policy achieves localization accuracy comparable to conventional GDOP-based placement, lowering the average localization Mean Absolute Error (MAE) by about 3 % relative to the stronger (mean-optimized) geometric baseline. The behavior is segment-dependent, with a clear improvement on individual segments (up to about 14 %) and comparable or slightly higher error on the others. These findings indicate that incorporating site-specific propagation data into the placement process can match and selectively improve upon purely geometric strategies, motivating further work toward consistent gains.
